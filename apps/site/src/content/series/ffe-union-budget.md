blurb: >-
  Every spring from the late 1960s onward the Forum of Free Enterprise put the
  Union Budget on trial before a Bombay audience and printed the verdict as a
  booklet. For three decades the voice was overwhelmingly Nani Palkhivala's,
  whose budget addresses filled auditoriums and then bookstalls; later years
  pass the task to H. P. Ranina, S. S. Bhandare and Minoo R. Shroff. Read in
  sequence the run is an unusually direct year-by-year record of how India's
  liberals read the fiscal state, from the high licence-permit era through
  liberalisation and after.
