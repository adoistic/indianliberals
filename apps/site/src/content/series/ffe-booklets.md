blurb: >-
  The longest continuous run in the archive. Founded in Bombay in 1956 by
  A. D. Shroff, the Forum of Free Enterprise argued the case for the market
  economy to a country then settling into planning, and it did so chiefly
  through a stream of slim saddle-stitched booklets — a lecture, a budget
  analysis, a panel discussion — printed cheaply and posted to members and
  student associates for a few rupees a year. Between 1956 and 2019 the Forum
  issued hundreds of them, drawing in Palkhivala, Masani, B. R. Shenoy, Colin
  Clark, Hayek and Friedman alongside bankers, industrialists and civil
  servants. The booklets are not serially numbered: the colophon on the back
  page carries the printing date rather than a booklet number, so the run is
  ordered chronologically.
