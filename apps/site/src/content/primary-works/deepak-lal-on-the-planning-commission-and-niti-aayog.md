summary: |-
  Deepak Lal recounts serving at the Planning Commission at the height of Indian planning, around 1973, when Indira Gandhi turned sharply left, nationalising coal, the banking system, and attempting to nationalise wholesale trade. He describes the meetings as absurd, citing a dispute among commission members over whether the growth target should be two or three percent, and notes that a proposal to nationalise wholesale trade was fortunately blocked.

  His decisive disillusionment came when he was asked to assess the impact of the first oil price shock and discovered the Perspective Planning Division's input-output table was based on roughly 1950 data, with no entries for oil or fertiliser, making it useless. He calls this his 'damascene conversion' and argues that anyone who spends a year at the Planning Commission and still believes in planning needs their head examined. On the NITI Aayog, he contends the government has merely changed the board outside while doing the same work, and suggests it should instead have set up something like a Council of Economic Advisers, given that funds now flow directly to the states.
