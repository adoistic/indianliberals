summary: |-
  In conversation, Zareer Masani argues that Jawaharlal Nehru began his premiership in a democratic, parliamentary spirit that accepted opponents, but that a cult of personality increasingly set in. He points to Nehru's residence in a grand palatial house, formerly the British Commander-in-Chief's and second only to the Viceroy's house, now the Nehru Museum and Library, where Nehru held state banquets and ran what Masani calls a Mughal-style imperial court, with Indira acting as consort and secretaries controlling access to him.

  Masani describes a growing dynastic tendency: Nehru's sister received plum diplomatic postings, including president of the UN General Assembly, High Commissioner in London, and ambassador in Moscow and Washington, while his daughter Indira, as her marriage to Feroz Gandhi fell apart, kept house for him and moved into politics, becoming Congress President in 1959 while he was Prime Minister. Nehru insisted the Congress Working Committee had simply chosen the best candidate, but Masani contrasts this with Nehru's grandfather barring his own son from a university benefit, arguing that Nehru's silence made it look as though he was promoting Indira as his heir amid succession anxieties over 'after Nehru who'.
