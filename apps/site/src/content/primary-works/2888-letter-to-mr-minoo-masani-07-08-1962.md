summary: |-
  This letter, written by Lok Sabha member P. K. Deo to M. R. Masani on 7 August 1962, reports on a 3 August meeting between Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Opposition leaders concerning recent Sino-Indian border developments east of Leh. Deo records Nehru’s assessment that Indian forces and civilians in Ladakh were seriously disadvantaged by Chinese numerical superiority, better roads, and stronger logistical access through Aksai Chin. Deo also notes Nehru’s view that India was not yet prepared to repel a Chinese attack and that China’s territorial ambitions extended beyond the approximately 20,000 square miles already at issue.
  
  Deo presents himself as pressing a more confrontational position than the Prime Minister. He says he advocated breaking diplomatic relations with China and exploring common security measures with Southeast Asian countries to protect democratic institutions from Communist regimes. According to Deo, Nehru dismissed the prospects for a regional security alliance, avoided directly responding to the proposal for severing relations, and remained reluctant to make categorical public commitments about territorial concessions. Deo concludes that the meeting produced little beyond a waste of time, while expressing confidence that Opposition members could expose the government’s weaknesses in the forthcoming parliamentary debate. The attached newspaper clippings report Nehru’s public account of the border situation, including claimed improvements in India’s Ladakh position and the presence of Opposition and defence officials at related briefings.

## Key points

- The letter summarizes a 3 August 1962 meeting between Nehru and Opposition leaders on the Sino-Indian border situation east of Leh.
- Deo reports that Indian personnel in Ladakh were outnumbered by Chinese forces by 15 to 1 and faced major communications and terrain disadvantages.
- He attributes to Nehru the view that India was not in a position to repel Chinese aggression and that China sought territory beyond the area already occupied.
- Deo advocated breaking diplomatic relations with China and criticized Nehru for not engaging directly with that proposal.
- Deo proposed common security measures with Southeast Asian countries to defend democratic institutions against Communist regimes.
- The letter portrays Nehru as unwilling to promise that India would sign no agreement involving territorial surrender, though still considering a possible visit to Peking.
- Deo says the meeting had no useful outcome and looks toward exposing the government’s position during the parliamentary debate.
- The attached press material records Nehru’s public claims about Indian defensive improvements in Ladakh and the absence of recent Chinese incursions in NEFA.
