summary: |-
  Deepak Lal calls the 1991 reforms crucial for dismantling much of the industrial-licensing, tariff, and delegated-planning apparatus and opening large space for private initiative, making business far easier than in the 1980s. But he argues the reforms were never carried through: bank nationalisation and the public-sector undertakings remain, and he criticises the government for not privatising loss-making Navratna enterprises, Air India, hotels, and steel mills, urging that their sale fund infrastructure such as roads and irrigation. He notes Modi's rhetoric that government has no business being in business, while faulting the gap with practice.

  On liberty more broadly, he attacks coercive legislation, recalling how the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act was used during the Emergency to jail people for holding a few dollars, and warning that the new 'black money law' will become a huge avenue for corruption and harassment by Indian Revenue Service officers. He argues personal liberty is not valued by Indian governments, citing censorship, 19th-century laws, the beef ban, threatened alcohol prohibition, and the criminalisation of homosexuality. Invoking Mill's essay On Liberty, he contrasts India with Ireland's recent legalisation of gay marriage despite a powerful Catholic Church, while crediting Modi with resisting some Hindu-first pressures.
