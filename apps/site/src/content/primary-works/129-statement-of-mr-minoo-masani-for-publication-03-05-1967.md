## Summary

This Swatantra Party statement, issued from its Bombay central office for publication, addresses the first presidential election contested under the Constitution. M. R. Masani rejects the claim that the defeat of Dr. Zakir Hussain would automatically bring down the Union government, explaining that the President is elected by an electoral college while the Cabinet remains responsible to the Lok Sabha. He presents the presidential contest as an opportunity to clarify the constitutional balance among the Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary, and argues that an independent President should exercise the office's constitutional functions rather than act as a mere rubber stamp.

## Key points

- Masani argues that the result of the presidential election cannot by itself determine the survival of the Union government.
- He defends the constitutional role of an independent President within a system of checks and balances.
- He rejects religious and communal criteria for judging the presidential candidates, presenting the Swatantra Party as committed to non-denominational democracy.
- He contrasts Dr. Zakir Hussain's association with the Congress Party with Mr. K. Subba Rao's independence and non-party background.
- He cites the Rajasthan constitutional crisis and the continuing Emergency as examples of situations in which presidential judgment matters.
- He denies that the Swatantra Party seeks presidential dictatorship and instead calls for parliamentary democracy with a stronger constitutional President.
- In the accompanying question-and-answer material, Masani supports opposition coordination without a formal bloc including Communists, reduced bureaucracy, lower non-productive government expenditure, and more clearly defined political parties.
