  Sagarika Ghose reads from Bruce Lawrence and David Gilmartin's book Beyond Turk and Hindu to argue that, before British colonial census-takers hardened religious categories, India contained a transactional, locally integrated world of Islamicate and Indic traditions. She frames these as liberal expressions of coexistence that can be recovered by studying India's religious traditions.

# Sagarika Ghose | Battle of Ideologies

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XHr4LZJSy5w
Duration: 122.1s

**Speaker 1** (00:00):
I'll read out a little bit from Bruce Lawrence and David Gilmartin because I think this is a very interesting book and I think it's important that you read this. It's called Beyond Turk and Hindu.

**Speaker 2** (00:14):
and they demonstrate that before the coming of the British colonial census takers, before identities were irrevocably cast as Muslim and Hindu, there was a transactional, local, patchily integrated world of Islamisate and Indic. This may not have been a secular idyll, but it was an interlinked space of memory, artifact and written text. This world created identities in which the categories of Hindu and Muslim were largely subsumed in more intermingled and localized structures of devotion, which combined traditions from all denominations.

**Speaker 1** (01:00):
So there was a tradition in India where these coexisting shrines had a long tradition and were deeply followed. I'm sure in your own homes you would know that there are certain saints and certain prophets who your family may worship or may be part of your neighborhood or may be part of your community who straddled both worlds. who straddled both the Hindu and the Islamic world. The most recent example of such a saint, of course, is the Sai Baba of Shirdi, who is worshipped equally by Hindus and Muslims, and according to some traditions, Sai Baba of Shirdi was, in fact, also a Muslim by birth. So, this is, again, another aspect of coexistence

**Speaker 2** (01:54):
and liberal expressions of coexistence that we can find if we study India's religious traditions.
