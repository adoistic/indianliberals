summary: |-
  The speakers recount how economist B.R. Shenoy coined the phrase "licence permit quota Raj," later popularised by Rajaji, and served on the Planning Commission with a reputation for independence and integrity that won him the presidency of the Indian Economic Association. They recall his 1958 Minute of Dissent against the Second Five Year Plan, arguing that with limited funds India should invest in light industry and agriculture, which generated far more jobs per unit of investment, rather than the Soviet-style heavy industry the government chose.

  Though the dissent reads mildly today, it was radical and courageous when Nehru's prestige was at its zenith and opposing him was almost unthinkable. Shenoy resigned and the committee was reconstituted without him, but his stand signalled to the outside world that India's path was not unanimously endorsed. The speakers relay an account, heard through the Forum of Free Enterprise, of Shenoy refusing an emissary's request to withdraw the note, and reflect on an era when people disagreed without being disagreeable. They note Shenoy was defeated in his time but vindicated by the 1991 reforms, which he did not live to see, having died around 1978.
