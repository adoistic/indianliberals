summary: |-
  Jayaprakash Narayan of the Lok Satta movement argues that effective civic activism requires fashioning concrete tools rather than merely protesting. His first major success, around 1998, targeted the roughly 10% short delivery of petrol at some 95% of India's fuel stations, which he attributes to tampered meters operating in collusion with state weights-and-measures departments. The tool was disarmingly simple: a pre-calibrated container of known volume that a consumer could ask to be filled instead of their vehicle's tank, making the cheating instantly provable. He claims that within fifteen days meters across the then Andhra Pradesh were corrected, and that the effort spurred oil companies to modernise their retail outlets, cutting short delivery from about 10% to around 1.5%.

  Narayan describes a parallel campaign against criminals in politics. His team set benchmarks and assembled a nonpartisan panel of eminent experts, including former chief justices and police officials, then solicited information from the media and public, receiving 2,600 responses. This produced a list of 42 criminal candidates, some very powerful and with murder charges, and helped shift the public climate, feeding into Supreme Court litigation, candidate disclosure norms and 'election watch' initiatives. He frames the need to invent such tools for citizen engagement as a blessing.
