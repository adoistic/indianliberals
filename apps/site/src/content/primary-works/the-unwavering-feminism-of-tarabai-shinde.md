  This explainer profiles Tarabai Shinde, a 19th-century social reformer born into an elite Maratha family in Buldana, Maharashtra. Though she received no formal schooling, she read widely and learned Marathi, English and Sanskrit at home under her father's guidance, and though forced into child marriage she held firm views on women's liberty. It recounts how the 1881 Vijayalakshmi case, in which a young pregnant widow from Surat was sentenced to life imprisonment for ending her pregnancy and was then attacked in the orthodox newspaper Pune Vaibhav, provoked her to respond.

The piece notes that she argued against child and forced marriage, defended the agency of all women from widows to sex workers, championed women's education as a route to critical thinking, and endured significant backlash while remaining influential today.
