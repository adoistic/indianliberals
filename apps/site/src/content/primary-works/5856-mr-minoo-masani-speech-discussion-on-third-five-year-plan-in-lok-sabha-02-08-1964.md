## Summary

In this Lok Sabha speech, M. R. Masani supports a substitute motion disapproving the Third Five Year Plan. He begins by identifying three conditions facing India: rising prices, stagnant incomes and worsening unemployment among agricultural and industrial workers, and a precarious external-financial position marked by declining foreign-exchange reserves and growing debt. Although he accepts the Plan's stated objectives—greater saving and investment, more efficient low-cost production, and exports—he argues that its actual policies undermine those objectives and benefit the ruling class and privileged monopolists rather than the wider population.

## Key points

- Masani presents inflation, stagnant incomes and unemployment, and foreign indebtedness as the central facts confronting India.
- He argues that excessive taxation, deficit financing, forced industrialisation, and high-cost heavy-industry projects make the Plan self-defeating.
- He criticises the expansion of the State sector and the displacement of private enterprise, claiming that production is being directed toward costly industries instead of food, raw materials, and consumer goods.
- He predicts further inflation, increased unemployment, reduced consumption, and a high-cost economy if the Plan is implemented.
- He attacks the Plan's pursuit of autarky and self-sufficiency, contrasting India's position with that of the Soviet Union and advocating continuing engagement with foreign capital and trade.
- He frames the Plan as a form of State capitalism rather than genuinely socialist economic development, because it may widen inequality and reduce popular consumption.
- He warns that collectivisation of land could threaten the peasantry and that excessive economic concentration could endanger constitutional and labour rights.
- He closes by describing the Plan as a threat to workers, peasants, and the middle class, and calls for educating the public about both living standards and economic freedom.
