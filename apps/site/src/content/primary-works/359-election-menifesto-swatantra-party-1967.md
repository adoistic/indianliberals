## Summary

The Swatantra Party’s 1967 Election Manifesto presents the party as an alternative to nearly two decades of Congress rule, which it portrays as responsible for constitutional erosion, economic stagnation, inflation, debt, administrative corruption, national disunity, and failures in foreign policy. It rejects both expanded authoritarian government and socialism-backed statism, arguing instead for individual freedom, private enterprise, dispersed ownership, competition, and government limited to a framework of good administration. The manifesto also claims that Swatantra’s parliamentary resistance has already checked collectivist farming, arbitrary land confiscation, excessive taxation, monopolies, inflationary policies, and administrative misconduct.

## Key points

- The manifesto attributes political, economic, moral, and foreign-policy disorder to uninterrupted Congress rule.
- It rejects dictatorship and statism in the name of socialism, advocating individual enterprise within a framework of good government.
- Its economic programme calls for lower taxation and government expenditure, abolition of land revenue and gold control, opposition to nationalisation, and restoration of competition.
- Agricultural policy combines secure tenancy, wider ownership, irrigation, credit, guaranteed floor prices, relief from indebtedness, and protection for landless labourers.
- The party proposes democratic rather than coercive planning, abolition of the Planning Commission, and rejection of the proposed Fourth Plan.
- The manifesto pledges restoration of Fundamental Rights, civil liberties, judicial access, administrative independence, electoral reform, and an Ombudsman.
- Its foreign-policy position criticises non-alignment, advocates regional security arrangements, and takes a strongly anti-Communist China position while addressing Pakistan and Kashmir.
- The programme extends to education, religious freedom, minority and backward-class protections, press independence, and a concluding appeal to Gandhian Dharma.
