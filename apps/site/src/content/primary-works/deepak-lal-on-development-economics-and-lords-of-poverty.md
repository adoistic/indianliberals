summary: |-
  Deepak Lal restates his long-standing claim that there is no separate subject called development economics, only mainstream economics, and laments that young economists are 'reinventing the wheel' by resurrecting discredited theories such as vicious circles of poverty and big pushes. He argues these theories are really instruments for fighting Western ideological battles in the Third World, with Africa the worst affected because, unlike India, China, or Latin America, it lacks a local core of trained economists able to challenge defunct ideas.

  He reserves his sharpest criticism for the World Bank, which he says was founded to finance reconstruction and infrastructure but was dismantled under James Wolfensohn in the 1990s in favour of gender and social issues where it has no expertise, leaving it redundant and, by refusing to finance dams and roads, a barrier to development. He praises China's new infrastructure bank for filling that gap and calls the aid establishment 'lords of poverty' who cannot afford to see poverty disappear. He criticises the IMF under Christine Lagarde for a milder version of the same drift, alleges the World Bank fiddled poverty figures to fit preconceived notions, and insists that actual poverty reduction over the past fifty years has been the fastest and largest in history, sustained despite predatory elites and foreign aid.
