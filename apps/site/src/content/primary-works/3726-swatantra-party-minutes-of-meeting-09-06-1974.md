summary: |-
  These draft minutes record the Swatantra Party General Council meeting held in New Delhi on 9–10 June 1974. The meeting was convened to consider the programme of a proposed national alternative formed through cooperation among non-Congress and non-Marxist parties. Members initially objected that the draft programme had not been circulated sufficiently in advance; Piloo Mody explained the compressed timetable and the negotiations that had produced the document. The discussion focused on whether the Swatantra Party should dissolve and join a new party, retain its identity within a federation, or continue independently while supporting electoral alliances.
  
  The minutes show a divided council. Supporters of dissolution argued that the party had declined electorally and organizationally, and that a larger national formation was necessary to preserve democratic competition. Opponents defended the Swatantra Party’s established identity and principles, questioned whether a coalition of parties with different ideologies could function effectively, and warned that dissolution might damage its reputation. Several speakers proposed intermediate arrangements, including a federal party, a minimum common programme, or a Swatantra Forum to preserve the party’s ideas. The attached resolutions welcomed efforts to create a national alternative but required that the final decision be taken at a National Convention after wider circulation and study of the draft programme. They also endorsed Jayaprakash Narayan’s non-violent movement in Bihar and protested the arrest of Ramender Kalia.

## Key points

- The General Council met to discuss a draft programme for a proposed national alternative to the ruling Congress.
- Members objected that the 24-page draft had not been circulated early enough for adequate study.
- Piloo Mody described negotiations among several parties and defended the proposal to dissolve the Swatantra Party before forming a new party.
- Supporters of dissolution cited the Swatantra Party’s shrinking membership, weakened public image, and poor electoral prospects.
- Opponents preferred a federation, electoral alliances, or continued Swatantra Party organization, stressing ideological and organizational differences among the proposed partners.
- The discussion repeatedly linked party reorganization to preserving democracy and checking the dominance of the ruling Congress.
- The resolutions deferred the final decision to a National Convention, welcomed the proposed national alternative, supported Jayaprakash Narayan’s Bihar movement, and protested Ramender Kalia’s arrest.
