    summary: "Masani sets out his personal creed in a series of short statements. He believes in the brotherhood of man, grounded in a shared human nature that responds to the same incentives everywhere, so that policies that work in Japan, Germany or the United States should work in India, and chauvinism is as reactionary as racism. He defines morality by the balance of happiness over misery, admits he cannot say whether such liberty would be exercised with restraint, and names Stoicism, especially Marcus Aurelius, as his philosophy. He traces his sympathy for the underdog to childhood, his early socialism to Wells and Shaw, and his renunciation of state socialism to arguments with Gandhi about ends and means, the origin of 'Socialism Reconsidered' (1944). He ranks freedom first among values, citing Lord Acton, and closes by calling heretics the salt of the earth and the Liberal a constructive agent of change who respects the good in tradition."

    summary: "Written in January 1994 for Freedom First, this open letter recalls that Masani and Rajaji founded the Swatantra Party in 1959 against the prevailing socialist dogma, and argues that Nehru's 'socialistic pattern of society' led to economic disaster and moral degradation. He notes that Narasimha Rao's Congress government admitted a crisis, with the country's gold pawned abroad, yet would not blame the model, and that the party had profited from Rajaji's 'permit-licence-quota-raj'. He defines liberalisation as the state leaving trade and industry to attend to law and order, drinking water, primary health, primary education and roads, which would end deficit financing and administered prices. Too frail to lead, he appeals to younger friends to organise a movement to ensure the reforms are honestly implemented, warning that otherwise India will again be at the bottom of the heap."

    author_resolved: s-v-raju
    summary: "S. V. Raju's tribute is a compact biography. It follows Masani from Elphinstone College, the LSE and Lincoln's Inn through jail in 1932-33, the Congress Socialist Party, and his disenchantment with Soviet communism after Stalin's purges, which led to resignation from the CSP and 'Socialism Reconsidered'. It records his imprisonment in the Quit India movement, mayoralty of Bombay in 1943, work in the Constituent Assembly, the 1947 'Plea for a Mixed Economy' lectures, and the clash with Nehru over minorities that sent him to Brazil as ambassador. Raju then covers the Indian Committee for Cultural Freedom, the Democratic Research Service, Masani's Lok Sabha years from 1957 to 1971, and the founding of the Swatantra Party with Rajaji in 1959. He credits Masani with growing the party to 44 Lok Sabha seats in 1967 and notes that Masani resigned after the 1971 defeat. The later sections cover his Emergency-era Freedom First case, the Minorities Commission, the Leslie Sawhny Programme, the Project for Economic Education, and the closing passages of his autobiography and We Indians."
