# The Emerging Scenario in Education

*The Eighth Nani A. Palkhivala Memorial Lecture — by Kapil Sibal*

## Summary

The Emerging Scenario in Education is the Eighth Nani A. Palkhivala Memorial Lecture, delivered extempore in Mumbai on 7 February 2011 by Kapil Sibal — then Union Minister for Human Resource Development, Communications and Information Technology — and published in June 2011 by the Nani A. Palkhivala Memorial Trust (c/o the Forum of Free Enterprise). In it Sibal sets out an ambitious vision to transform Indian education within a decade, premised on the conviction that every child matters and that the most disadvantaged must have access to the best education if people at the "bottom of the pyramid" are to be empowered.

On the quantitative side, Sibal's stated goal is to roughly treble the share of 18–24-year-olds in higher education — from about 12.4 per cent to 30 per cent — by adding some 50,000 colleges and 1,000 universities. But the lecture stresses that numbers are not enough: he advances far-reaching proposals on quality, including replacing the school examination system with continuous and comprehensive evaluation, "democracy in the classroom" that gives students greater freedom to develop their own interests, interconnectivity between colleges so a student in one can access lectures in another, a greatly increased emphasis on research, and vocational training for the large numbers who will not reach college. Acknowledging widespread malpractice in the existing system, he proposes to open education to the private sector while building a fair, transparent, quality-based accreditation regime, backed by a suite of legislative measures — the lecture being, in the Trust's framing, his reasoned exposition of the compulsions behind those Bills.

As with every lecture in the series, the published booklet frames Sibal's address with a memorial tribute to Nani A. Palkhivala (1920–2002) — jurist, defender of constitutional liberties, long-serving President of the Forum of Free Enterprise, and celebrated expositor of the Union Budget — situating a sitting minister's education-reform agenda within the Forum's tradition of public debate on economic and civic questions.

## Key points

- The Eighth Nani A. Palkhivala Memorial Lecture, delivered extempore by Union HRD Minister Kapil Sibal in Mumbai on 7 February 2011; published June 2011 by the Nani A. Palkhivala Memorial Trust.

- Sets a ten-year goal to raise higher-education enrolment among 18–24-year-olds from ~12.4% to 30%, adding roughly 50,000 colleges and 1,000 universities.

- Proposes replacing school examinations with continuous and comprehensive evaluation and introducing "democracy in the classroom" — more student freedom to pursue their interests.

- Calls for interconnectivity between colleges (cross-institution access to lectures) and a greatly increased emphasis on research.

- Would open education to the private sector while instituting fair, transparent, quality-based accreditation, and expand vocational training for those who do not reach college.

- Frames these goals as the rationale behind a package of education legislation introduced under his stewardship, including the Right to Education.

- The booklet situates a Congress minister's reform agenda within the Forum of Free Enterprise's tradition, prefaced by a tribute to Palkhivala as jurist and champion of constitutional liberty.
