## Summary

This single-page English newspaper clipping, identified in handwriting as from the Sunday Standard dated 16 April 1972, reports from Gwalior on a feared and respected figure associated with the Chambal valley's bandit problem. The article describes him as a tall, gaunt man with a large moustache and says that he claims to have encountered the message of non-violence through Vinoba Bhave. The visible text presents him as advocating the surrender of dacoits rather than a purely coercive solution.

The clipping argues, in the visible passages, that negotiations with bandit leaders might offer a way forward in the dacoit-infested Chambal region. It says that attempts to persuade Central and State governments to consider surrender have been frustrated by cynical politicians, while also suggesting that the proposal could be effective. The headline and parts of the article are obscured by an advertisement, and no byline is visible.

- It profiles a tall, gaunt man with a large moustache who is described as feared and respected in the valley.
- The profiled figure links his commitment to non-violence to Vinoba Bhave.
- He advocates persuading dacoit leaders to surrender.
- The article says that Central and State governments have been urged to consider surrender.
- The visible text attributes delays or frustration to cynical politicians.
- The clipping proposes negotiation with bandit leaders as a potentially effective response.
