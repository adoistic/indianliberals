summary: |-
  This is a promotional introduction to the Hindi translation of a primer on public choice theory, framed around a single core insight: the assumption that self-interest stops operating once individuals enter government is false. The speakers argue that politicians, voters, bureaucrats and regulators all pursue their own interests within the political system, just as buyers and sellers do in the market. From this it follows that we cannot assume a benevolent, omniscient government will improve on market outcomes.

  The talk contends that self-interest in politics produces "government failure" that can be more damaging than market failure, because government wields coercive power and faces no direct competitive check. In a representative democracy, the self-interest of a small group can drive decisions harmful to the majority. The primer is presented as exploring how political systems might be designed with balance and restraint, confining collective action to areas where it is genuinely desirable.
