## Summary

This two-page file preserves a pair of express telegrams sent by Minoo Masani from the Swatantra Party's central office at 143 Mahatma Gandhi Road, Bombay, on 22 January 1963 to N.G. Ranga MP at Western Court, New Delhi. The exchange is a party-line coordination note on the eve of Parliament's debate over the Colombo proposals — the six-nation mediation formula that emerged from the December 1962 Colombo conference on the Sino-Indian border war — and over a Constitution Amendment Bill then before the House.

The first telegram, endorsing a letter from Rajaji to Ranga written the previous day, urges that Swatantra MPs — jointly or separately — move an amendment reaffirming the last paragraph of the party's November fourteenth resolution and categorically rejecting the Colombo proposals. Masani warns that if such an amendment is not pressed to a vote, the Government will later be able to claim it had the unanimous permission of the House to negotiate, and he flatly dismisses a walk-out unaccompanied by an amendment as "meaningless." The second, crossing telegram tells Ranga that Masani's view on the Colombo proposals has already gone out in an earlier wire; on the Constitution Bill, however, he passes on the Parliamentary Board's decision that Swatantra MPs are to abstain from both the debate and the vote, and asks that this ruling be respected. A confirmatory copy of the first telegram was sent by Express Delivery to C. Rajagopalachari at Bazlulah Road, T. Nagar, Madras.

## Key points

- Two express telegrams from Minoo Masani to N.G. Ranga MP, both dated 22 January 1963, sent from the Swatantra Party's central office in Bombay.
- First wire endorses a Rajaji letter to Ranga of 21 January and calls for Swatantra MPs to move an amendment rejecting the Colombo proposals on the Sino-Indian border dispute.
- Masani insists the amendment must be pressed to a vote, arguing that its absence would let the Government later claim a unanimous House mandate to negotiate.
- He rules out a bare walk-out without a recorded vote, calling such a protest 'meaningless'.
- Second wire, crossing Ranga's own telegram of the same day, refers back to the Colombo message and turns to the Constitution Amendment Bill before the House.
- On the Constitution Bill, Masani conveys the Parliamentary Board's binding decision that Swatantra MPs abstain from both debate and voting, and asks Ranga to respect it.
- A confirmatory copy of the first telegram is directed by Express Delivery to C. Rajagopalachari in Madras, evidencing tight Masani–Rajaji coordination of the Swatantra whip.
