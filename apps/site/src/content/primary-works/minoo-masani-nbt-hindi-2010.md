summary: "Minoo Masani (मीनू मसानी) is the National Book Trust's Hindi edition, in its 'राष्ट्रीय जीवनचरित' (National Biography) series, of S. V. Raju's biography of Minoo Masani (1905-1998); the work is a translation of an English original, rendered into Hindi by the poet Saumitra Mohan (सौमित्र मोहन), and the copyright page identifies it as 'Minoo Masani (Hindi)'. Masani is the subject, not the author. The preface opens by calling the book a life of 'a politician of principle' (सिद्धांतवादी राजनीतिज्ञ), and the twelve chapters follow his life in order: a Parsi family background and legal education at the London School of Economics; the Gandhian civil-disobedience years, with three jail terms (1932, 1933, 1943) and the Quit India underground; a decade as a Marxist and a founder of the Congress Socialist Party; the break with Soviet communism after his 1937 visit and his resignation from the CSP in 1939; his 1934 padyatra with Gandhi and the essay Socialism Reconsidered (1944); and sixteen years as a Tata executive. The later chapters take up his membership of the Indian Legislative Assembly and Constituent Assembly, his widening rift with Nehru, the founding of the Swatantra Party with C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) in 1959, its rise and its dissolution in 1974, his conduct as a parliamentarian, and his last phase, which covers J. P. Narayan's movement, the Emergency, the Minorities Commission and the final books."
summary_structured:
  key_points:
    - "The book presents Masani as a principled liberal who spent most of his life in opposition. Raju's preface, helped by a tribute from J. R. D. Tata and a quotation from R. Srinivasan, argues that his intellectual honesty and refusal to compromise on principle cost him political success, and that this is the reason he deserves remembering. The author worked beside Masani from December 1959 as office secretary of the Swatantra Party's central office and later as a manager of Masani's voluntary bodies, so the portrait is that of a close associate."
    - "On Masani's intellectual path, the biography traces a movement from Marx to Gandhi. He was a committed socialist and admirer of the Soviet Union, and visited it in 1927 and again in 1935/37. His Soviet Sidelights was largely admiring, with doubts noted. Communist infiltration of the CSP under the 'united front' policy, the Stalin-Hitler pact and the purges disillusioned him. He resigned from the CSP in 1939 and then campaigned against communism (the Democratic Research Service, 1950, and the Indian Committee for Cultural Freedom, 1951)."
    - "Gandhi's influence is given a full chapter. During the ten-day Orissa padyatra of 1934 Gandhi answered Masani's questionnaire on socialism, nationalisation, zamindari and foreign trade in terms Masani at first found 'reactionary' and later adopted. The lessons Masani drew include the distinction between a system and the people who run it, the point that means and ends are linked, and the case for decentralisation. The book argues that these ideas shaped his later liberalism and the principles of Swatantra."
    - "In Socialism Reconsidered (1944) he questioned four Marxist assumptions: that nationalisation brings a classless society, that the dictatorship of the proletariat is a necessary transition, that appeals to the workers' collective self-interest can build socialism, and that capitalism and socialism are the only alternatives. In 1947 he argued for a mixed economy in three sectors: a very small nationalised one, new public enterprises, and a large free-enterprise sector. The book returns often to his maxim that you cannot replace 'something' with 'nothing'."
    - "The Tata years (1941-1956) are shown as practical liberalism. He helped introduce workers' participation in management at TISCO, Jamshedpur (1956), after study visits to West Germany and Yugoslavia, and he organised the 1965 international seminar that produced a declaration on the social responsibility of business. The chapter also records his insistence on earning his own living ('आजीविका'), which explains his several withdrawals from politics."
    - "Chapters 8 and 9 cover the Legislative and Constituent Assembly years. They include his advocacy of a uniform civil code, of proportional representation instead of reserved seats, and of the abolition of titles. They also cover his clashes with Nehru over the 1947 Liaquat Ali budget, the Soviet Union and the Cold War, and the Nagpur co-operative-farming resolution of 1959, which the book names as the trigger for Swatantra. Raju calls Rajaji the architect and Masani the builder of the party."
    - "The book gives Swatantra's organisational record: Masani as general secretary and then president, five national conventions, 44 Lok Sabha seats and the largest opposition bloc in 1967, then the 1971 'Grand Alliance' disaster over the 'Indira Hatao' slogan, his resignation, and the party's winding up in August 1974. A separate chapter shows his parliamentary craft: respect for the Speaker, short speeches, chairing the Public Accounts Committee (1967-69), and a regular constituency routine for Rajkot."
    - "The last chapter takes in the J. P. movement and the Emergency. It records Masani's warnings about the cost of confrontation, his fight against pre-censorship of Freedom First, which Soli Sorabjee argued before the Bombay High Court, and his refusal to join the opposition coordination committee. It then covers his three-month chairmanship of the Minorities Commission (1978), his late projects (the Society for the Right to Die with Dignity, the economic-education arm of the Leslie Sawhny Programme, We the Indians, 1989) and his death in May 1998. A bibliography of his books closes the volume."

# मीनू मसानी

## Summary

Minoo Masani (मीनू मसानी) is the National Book Trust's Hindi edition, in its 'राष्ट्रीय जीवनचरित' (National Biography) series, of S. V. Raju's biography of Minoo Masani (1905-1998); the work is a translation of an English original, rendered into Hindi by the poet Saumitra Mohan (सौमित्र मोहन), and the copyright page identifies it as 'Minoo Masani (Hindi)'. Masani is the subject, not the author. The preface opens by calling the book a life of 'a politician of principle' (सिद्धांतवादी राजनीतिज्ञ), and the twelve chapters follow his life in order: a Parsi family background and legal education at the London School of Economics; the Gandhian civil-disobedience years, with three jail terms (1932, 1933, 1943) and the Quit India underground; a decade as a Marxist and a founder of the Congress Socialist Party; the break with Soviet communism after his 1937 visit and his resignation from the CSP in 1939; his 1934 padyatra with Gandhi and the essay Socialism Reconsidered (1944); and sixteen years as a Tata executive. The later chapters take up his membership of the Indian Legislative Assembly and Constituent Assembly, his widening rift with Nehru, the founding of the Swatantra Party with C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) in 1959, its rise and its dissolution in 1974, his conduct as a parliamentarian, and his last phase, which covers J. P. Narayan's movement, the Emergency, the Minorities Commission and the final books.
