summary: |-
  This six-page English typescript records excerpts from a July 9 discussion in Bombay in which activists questioned Jayaprakash Narayan about family planning, Janata Party integration, ministerial conduct, labour unrest, language policy, and grassroots democratic organisation. On family planning, Narayan acknowledges that the Emergency-era methods associated with Sanjay had discredited the programme, but maintains that population control remains necessary and calls for new, voluntary approaches capable of gaining public acceptance.
  
  On Janata politics, Narayan advises dissatisfied workers to remain patient while the four constituent parties and their youth wings develop a unified identity. He argues that democratic pressures, including the importance of Harijan and minority votes, should moderate the Jan Sangh elements within the party. He similarly counsels against abrupt public attacks on new Janata ministers, while recommending constructive criticism. On labour unrest, he attributes some anger to feelings suppressed during the Emergency but insists that government must not allow the situation to drift. The discussion closes with support for English as a link language rather than the medium of instruction, greater attention to Hindi, and People's Committees that continuously monitor elected representatives. The document's handwritten corrections and variant question-and-answer pages indicate that it circulated as an edited or revised typescript.

## Key points

- Narayan argues that family planning needs voluntary, socially differentiated methods after the programme's discrediting during the Emergency.
- He urges patience as the Congress, Jan Sangh, Bharatiya Lok Dal, and Socialist Party elements attempt to fuse within the Janata Party.
- He expects democratic accountability and the political weight of Harijans and minorities to moderate Jan Sangh elements.
- He attributes the unchanged style of Janata ministers partly to inherited civil-service practices and their limited time in office.
- He links labour unrest to anger bottled up during the Emergency but calls for decisive government action in the country's interest.
- He supports English as a language and link language, not as the medium of school instruction, while endorsing Hindi's wider development.
- He presents People's Committees and Gram Sabhas as continuous grassroots mechanisms for monitoring representatives and advancing socio-economic reform.
