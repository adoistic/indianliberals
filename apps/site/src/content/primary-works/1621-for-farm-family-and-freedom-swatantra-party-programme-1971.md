## Summary

This 1971 Swatantra Party election manifesto presents a programme centred on constitutional government, fundamental rights, law and order, employment, agricultural prosperity, and economic growth. Part I defends the Constitution and judicial role, warns against governmental encroachment on personal property and liberties, and proposes large public-works programmes, rural housing and infrastructure, support for family farming and peasant proprietorship, land-tenure protections, stable money, lower administrative costs, and a less restrictive regulatory framework for trade and industry. It also calls for protections for minorities and backward classes, a Commission on Civil Rights, and equal opportunity in education and employment.

The manifesto’s conclusion frames the programme as a call to remove the existing government and reconstruct the country through individual initiative, enterprise, productive work, and protection for the poor. Part II reproduces an All India Congress Committee document dated June 28, 1970, which describes a political crisis involving violence, communal and linguistic disputes, threats to constitutional liberties, economic stagnation, and declining standards of public life. The Congress text advocates national unity, constitutional democracy, social and economic justice, employment programmes, agricultural development, autonomous public-sector management, anti-corruption institutions, regulated foreign capital, and collective bargaining. The document therefore preserves two competing election-era programme statements: Swatantra’s liberal critique of planning and regulation, and Congress’s account of democratic stability and developmental governance.

## Key points

- The Swatantra Party presents constitutional protection, fundamental rights, judicial independence, and law and order as prerequisites for stable democracy.
- The manifesto identifies unemployment, inflation, faulty planning, and excessive administrative expenditure as central economic failures.
- It proposes public works, rural housing, roads, irrigation, electrification, drinking water, and targeted employment programmes.
- It defends peasant proprietorship and family farming while supporting land reform, security of tenure, fair rents, minimum wages, crop insurance, and agricultural support prices.
- It advocates stable currency, incentives for private enterprise, reduced red tape, autonomous administration of permits and licences, competition, and limited regulation of unavoidable monopolies.
- It calls for equality of opportunity, protection of minorities and backward classes, anti-discrimination measures, and a Commission on Civil Rights.
- The reproduced Congress text shares commitments to constitutional liberties, employment, rural development, education, anti-corruption, and minority protection, but places them within a more explicitly developmental and party-political framework.
