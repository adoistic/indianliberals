summary: |
  Theoretical Vision is a collection of the theoretical writings of B R Shenoy on economics, economic development, savings, money, inflation, prices, currency and banking. Most of the theoretical writings in the volume date from 1932 to 1953, covering the period when Shenoy was studying at the London School of Economics and the early years of India's economic development. The volume brings together his writings on the foundations of a free society, economic progress, India's planning experience, savings and monetary policy. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}
  
  The book is divided into four broad sections: Economic Constituents of a Free Society, Savings, Money Management, and Gold/Currency/Banking. It includes writings such as "Economic constituents of a free society", "Measures of economic progress", "25 years of planning", "Sources of finance for economic expansion", "Indian national savings", "International investment gap", "Dangers of inflationary finance", "Price policy", "Economics of corruption", and essays on the gold standard, currency and the rupee. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1} :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
  
  A central concern running through the volume is the relationship between economic freedom, savings, monetary stability and economic development. Shenoy examines the limits of deficit finance and artificially created credit, arguing that lasting economic progress requires genuine savings and sound economic institutions. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
