summary: |-
  This six-page English letter, dated 28 March 1971 and issued from the Swatantra Party’s Coorg Unit, is addressed to N. K. Ganapaiah after the party’s poor performance in the parliamentary election. Rajkumar R. Ghorpade attributes the defeat not to the party’s principles or programme but to a distorted public image and, especially, the absence of strong grassroots organisation. He argues that the Swatantra Party must accept responsibility, improve its public appeal without abandoning its principles, and prepare to replace both Congress and the Communist Party as a national political alternative.
  
  The letter proposes a more concrete “People’s Programme” directed at the immediate concerns of the majority: rising prices, unemployment, self-employment, rural development, drinking water, housing, communications, education, health, cottage industries, and labour welfare. Ghorpade also insists that democracy, fundamental and human rights, judicial independence, property rights, and the rule of law should become more visible elements of the party’s public philosophy. Its organisational proposals include dedicated state organisers, regular visits by national leaders, regional-language supplements, and systematic recruitment across social groups. The letter closes by warning that the party must either build a nationwide, disciplined organisation or risk existing only as a marginal force.

## Key points

- The Swatantra Party’s 1971 electoral defeat in Mysore State is attributed to a distorted public image and weak grassroots organisation rather than to its principles or programme.
- The party is urged to take full responsibility for the result instead of blaming coalition partners or individual candidates.
- A clear and accessible People’s Programme should address prices, unemployment, self-employment, rural development, housing, education, health, communications, cottage industries, and labour welfare.
- Democracy, fundamental rights, human rights, judicial independence, property rights, and the rule of law should be made more prominent in the party’s public presentation.
- The party needs a permanent organisational structure involving state organisers, national leadership tours, youth workers, local units, and constituency-level activity.
- Regional-language supplements and independent weekly publications are proposed as inexpensive ways to reach sympathisers and the wider public.
- The author argues that the party must expand beyond selected pockets and choose between systematic development and gradual decline.
