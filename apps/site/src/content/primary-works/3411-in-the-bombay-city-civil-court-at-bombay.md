## Summary

This 1974 civil-court plaint, filed in the Bombay City Civil Court, sets out the plaintiffs’ account of an internal dispute concerning the Swatantra Party, its constitution, assets, organisational structure, and proposed dissolution or merger into a new political party. The rendered pages identify 32 defendants as former members or office-bearers of the Swatantra Party and describe the formation of the Bharatiya Lok Dal in August 1974. The pleading argues that the Swatantra Party’s 1959 statement of principles and constitution established its political identity and governed the authority of its various organisational units.

The pages then document the party’s membership rules, subscription system, allocation of funds, national and regional structures, disciplinary powers, conventions, amendment procedures, and the handling of party property and offices. They also reproduce or summarise resolutions and correspondence from 1974 concerning a proposed national alternative and a two-party system. The plaintiffs contend that the proposed new party was insufficiently ideological and that dissolving the Swatantra Party would abandon its foundational principles, while the cited programme presents a platform focused on nation-building, administrative efficiency, anti-corruption, social justice, and a broad political alternative to the Congress Party. The rendered material ends during the account of disputes over delegate quotas and the Maharashtra State Council’s opposition to dissolving the Swatantra Party; the pleading continues beyond the pages shown.

## Key points

- The document is a 1974 Bombay civil-court plaint concerning the Swatantra Party and a proposed successor or merger arrangement.
- The plaintiffs identify the Swatantra Party’s 1959 principles and constitution as the foundation of its political identity.
- The pleading describes a hierarchical party organisation consisting of primary, district, state or regional, general-council, and national-executive bodies.
- Party membership, subscriptions, finances, elections, disciplinary procedures, appeals, and constitutional amendments are presented in detail.
- The plaintiffs distinguish the Swatantra Party’s established principles from a proposed new party described as primarily programme-based.
- The cited political programme advocates broad mobilisation, practical governance, anti-corruption, poverty reduction, and a two-party system.
- The Maharashtra State Council is described as opposing the dissolution of the Swatantra Party and questioning the proposed national alternative.
