## Summary

This one-page typewritten letter, dated 23rd February 1963 and marked 'Express Delivery', is written by M. R. Masani to C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) reporting on the successful placement of a coordinated political statement across India's major English-language dailies. Masani credits 'the kind cooperation of two or three of our perennial friends' with securing paid space in all five newspapers that Rajaji had specified — the Hindu, the Times of India (Bombay), the Hindustan Times, the Statesman (Calcutta), and the southern edition of the Indian Express — and sets out the publication schedule between 25th and 27th February.

A caution follows the good news: only the Hindu has confirmed acceptance of the text; the Statesman and the Times of India are still 'having a look' before deciding whether to run the piece as an advertisement rather than as an ordinary insertion. Masani conveys confidence that all will 'come clean' in the end, and confirms the physical layout Rajaji had requested — a half page in bold type, horizontal across the sheet rather than vertical, prefaced by Rajaji's personal appeal.

A postscript notes that Rajaji's letter of 20th February has just arrived and remarks that the source of Rajaji's depression — apparently linked to the byelections — 'has already been dissipated', promising a further update on Monday and mentioning an article by Kripalani that Rajaji should by now have received. The letter is a small but telling document of Swatantra-era political communication: paid, coordinated, multi-city press advocacy negotiated one editor at a time.

## Key points

- Masani writes to Rajaji on 23 February 1963 confirming that paid space has been secured in all five newspapers Rajaji had asked for, including the southern edition of the Indian Express.
- The coordinated publication schedule spans 25th–27th February across the Hindu, Times of India (Bombay), Hindustan Times, Statesman (Calcutta), and Indian Express.
- Only the Hindu has firmly accepted the text; the Statesman and Times of India are reviewing it and may insist on running it as an advertisement rather than a news insertion.
- The layout follows Rajaji's specification: half a page in bold type, horizontal across the sheet, prefaced by Rajaji's personal appeal.
- A postscript acknowledges Rajaji's letter of 20 February and reports that his 'depression' about the byelections has already been dissipated; Masani promises a further update after weekend reflection.
- Masani also references an article by Kripalani that Rajaji should have received by now, indicating an ongoing exchange of political commentary between the correspondents.
- The letter documents the operational mechanics of Swatantra-adjacent public advocacy in the early 1960s — how a coordinated national newspaper appeal was actually procured and placed.
