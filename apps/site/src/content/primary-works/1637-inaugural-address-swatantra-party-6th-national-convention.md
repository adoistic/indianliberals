## Summary

In this inaugural address to the Swatantra Party’s Sixth National Convention in Madras on 14–15 April 1973, M. R. Masani reviews the political developments since the party’s previous convention in 1968. He criticises the Congress split, the failure to construct an opposition bloc, the 1971 election system and mandate, and the concentration of power under Indira Gandhi. He argues that the 24th and 25th Constitutional Amendments, restrictions on the press and academic institutions, and attacks on trade-union freedom threaten fundamental rights, constitutional government and the rule of law. The address also presents a moral critique of political opportunism and corruption.

## Key points

- Masani presents the Swatantra Party as seeking a credible alternative government through cooperation among democratic opposition parties.
- He argues that the 1971 Congress victory was not a popular landslide but an artefact of an electoral system that converted a minority vote into an overwhelming parliamentary majority.
- The address links the 24th and 25th Constitutional Amendments and restrictions on the press, films, books, universities and trade unions to a wider assault on civil liberties.
- Masani attributes India’s economic difficulties to population growth, unemployment, inflation, deficit financing, nationalisation and excessive concentration on state-owned heavy industry.
- He challenges official agricultural and land-reform policies, arguing that ceiling laws and moves toward collective or state farming weaken incentives and threaten peasant proprietorship.
- He criticises government monopolies in coal, foodgrain and other commodities as inefficient and harmful to consumers and small traders.
- In foreign policy, he objects to India’s close alignment with the Soviet Union and calls for a more independent and balanced position among the major powers.
- The address concludes with a programme of lower taxation, reduced unproductive expenditure, monetary stability, labour-intensive development, competition, fewer controls and a return to pragmatic middle-of-the-road government.
