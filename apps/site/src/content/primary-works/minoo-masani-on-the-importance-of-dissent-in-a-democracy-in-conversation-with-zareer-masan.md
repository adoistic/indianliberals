summary: |-
  In conversation, Zareer Masani remembers his father Minoo Masani as a leader of the parliamentary opposition whose annual reply to the Union budget was a major Lok Sabha event, marked by surgical, economically incisive attacks on the government of Jawaharlal Nehru and finance ministers T.T. Krishnamachari and Morarji Desai. He stresses that although Masani and Nehru clashed sharply and their rivalry dated to their student days, the disagreements stayed gentlemanly and never personal, recalling Nehru warmly greeting his family at Palam Airport.

  Masani also led opposition attacks on Nehru's China policy, arguing along with Rajaji and Jayaprakash Narayan that India should have opposed China's takeover of Tibet. Zareer describes how the 1962 defeat left Nehru weakened and forced Krishna Menon's resignation, and recounts Masani's hope that a Nehru-Ayub Khan rapprochement over Kashmir, brokered via the released Sheikh Abdullah, might succeed, an effort cut short by Nehru's death and followed by the 1965 war.
