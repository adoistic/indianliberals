## Summary

The Swatantra Party’s 1969 Mid-Term Report presents itself as a political balance sheet for April 1967–September 1969, assessing the party’s performance since the 1967 general elections and outlining the work it proposes before the 1972 elections. The rendered pages describe substantial electoral gains: the party doubled its Lok Sabha strength from 22 to 44, became the single largest Opposition group in both Houses of Parliament, won 259 seats in state assemblies, and led a coalition government in Orissa. The report stresses the party’s rural base, noting that 98 per cent of its state legislators represented rural constituencies and that 42 of its 44 Lok Sabha members came from rural or semi-urban constituencies.

The report records Swatantra’s parliamentary and state-level opposition to higher taxes, increased railway and postal rates, bank nationalisation, the Essential Commodities Act, centralised planning, food zones, the Gold Control Act, and other regulatory measures. It claims that several party positions had influenced subsequent policy, including the creation of the Lok Pal and Lok Ayukt, movement toward delicensing and decontrol, and changes in planning and administrative reform. The Orissa section highlights the Swatantra-led coalition’s reported achievements in land-revenue abolition, decentralisation, agricultural and industrial development, administrative economies, anti-corruption measures, and communal-peace efforts, while acknowledging limitations in public relations and policy dynamism.

The report is also self-critical. It attributes disappointing by-election results and weak performance in the 1968–69 mini-general elections to poor organisation, inadequate communication of party policy, insufficient field work, conflicting statements by leaders, limited resources, and failures to apply its principles consistently. The final balance-sheet discussion judges the party’s parliamentary and governmental record favourably but concludes that its organisation outside legislatures has failed to sustain the enthusiasm generated by the 1967 elections. The supplied pages end during this organisational assessment; the report’s later proposals and concluding material were not rendered.

## Key points

- The report evaluates the Swatantra Party’s electoral, parliamentary, state-assembly, and governmental record from April 1967 to September 1969.
- The party doubled its Lok Sabha strength from 22 to 44 and became the largest Opposition group in both Houses.
- The report emphasises Swatantra’s rural social base and presents its legislators as representatives of peasants and other relatively neglected groups.
- The party describes opposition to planning, nationalisation, higher taxation, price controls, food zones, and other forms of state regulation.
- The Swatantra-led Orissa coalition is credited with administrative decentralisation, land-revenue abolition, industrial development, anti-corruption initiatives, and communal-peace measures.
- The report claims that government adopted or moved toward several policies previously advocated by Swatantra, including an ombudsman institution and industrial delicensing.
- The party acknowledges organisational weakness, inadequate public communication, conflicting leadership statements, limited resources, and inconsistent adherence to its own principles.
- The visible pages present the report as broadly favourable to the party’s legislative and governmental record but sharply critical of its extra-legislative organisation.
