summary: |-
  This Swatantra Party circular, signed by General Secretary R. C. Cooper and addressed to parliamentary candidates, supplies campaign speaking points and reproduced press extracts. The material attacks the Congress government, especially its handling of the Kendu-leaf affair in Orissa, its alleged obstruction of an anti-corruption inquiry, and what the circular presents as the politically motivated resignation of Jana Congress ministers. It also argues that Prime Minister Indira Gandhi’s electoral strategy combines radical rhetoric with increasingly authoritarian use of administrative power, while opposition parties have failed to offer a sufficiently clear democratic alternative.
  
  A substantial section challenges India’s inherited model of socialism. It contrasts “dogmatic” or “outmoded” socialism—associated with nationalisation, state ownership, concentrated economic power, heavy taxation, and excessive regulation—with a proposed “20th Century Socialism” based on private enterprise, reduced controls, progressive taxation, economic growth, and welfare. The circular compares India with developing countries in South-East Asia and claims that these countries have achieved better results by treating private enterprise as compatible with socialism. Additional extracts use ministerial salaries and perks, and an idealised account of Switzerland’s modest presidency, to contrast political privilege with restrained public service. The final pages urge voters to look past campaign personalities and choose between economic radicalism and financial stability, while questioning the Prime Minister’s use of official resources during the election campaign.

## Key points

- The circular frames the Kendu-leaf controversy and the Orissa ministry crisis as evidence of political manoeuvring and dishonesty.
- It criticises Indira Gandhi’s combination of socialist rhetoric, administrative power, and alleged authoritarian practice.
- It rejects India’s state-centred and nationalisation-oriented socialism as economically ineffective and socially inequitable.
- It advocates a practical “20th Century Socialism” combining private enterprise, limited regulation, progressive taxation, and public welfare.
- It compares India unfavourably with developing South-East Asian countries that rely more heavily on private production and investment.
- It presents ministerial salaries, allowances, housing, vehicles, and tax treatment as evidence of a gap between socialist claims and political privilege.
- It uses Switzerland’s relatively modest, accessible presidency as a model of restrained and service-oriented government.
- It urges candidates and voters to identify substantive electoral issues, particularly the choice between economic radicalism and financial stability.
