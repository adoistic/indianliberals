## Summary

In this presidential address to the Swatantra Party’s Fifth National Convention in Bhubaneswar, N. G. Ranga begins with the immediate rural crises of drought, famine, floods, inadequate water supply, and poor housing. He calls for a permanent All-India Famine and Flood Protection Fund, crop and cattle insurance, expanded rural water infrastructure, and a national housing programme. He also links these material concerns to elementary education, adult literacy, the condition of Harijans, Adivasis, agricultural workers, and other underprivileged groups, arguing that social welfare and basic rights require sustained public action rather than episodic relief or election promises.

The address then turns to constitutionalism and foreign policy. Ranga defends Fundamental Rights against amendment, condemns the Soviet invasion of Czechoslovakia, and argues that India has become dangerously dependent on the Soviet Union. He advocates peace with Pakistan, opposition to an arms race, and rejection of atomic-bomb development. In its economic and political programme, the speech presents the Swatantra Party as a defender of self-employment, property, competition, and a non-exploitative society against centralised planning, state monopolies, excessive controls, and inefficient public enterprises. The later pages criticise Congress rule and unstable United Front ministries while proposing broader democratic cooperation among non-Communist opposition parties. ## Key points

- Ranga presents drought, famine, floods, and rural deprivation as national emergencies requiring permanent institutions rather than temporary relief.
- He proposes an All-India Famine and Flood Protection Fund, crop and cattle insurance, rural water projects, and expanded housing provision.
- The address links elementary education and adult literacy to the advancement of Harijans, Adivasis, agricultural workers, and other disadvantaged groups.
- Ranga defends Fundamental Rights and the constitutional protection of individual liberty against parliamentary amendment.
- He condemns Soviet domination of Czechoslovakia and criticises the Indian government’s alleged drift into Soviet satellitism.
- The speech advocates India-Pakistan reconciliation, a No-War Pact, arms limitation, and continued opposition to nuclear weapons.
- Ranga defines a non-exploitative society through self-employment, property rights, competition, trusteeship, and protection from state or private monopoly.
- He attacks centralised planning, permit-quota controls, nationalisation, and inefficient public enterprises while calling for democratic opposition unity.
