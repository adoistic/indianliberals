## Summary

In this five-page pamphlet, M. R. Masani explains the Swatantra Party’s opposition to the nationalisation of fourteen Indian banks. He argues that the Government had not demonstrated a public-interest case, that less drastic measures such as social control were available, and that nationalisation would either be pointless and expensive or politically motivated and dangerous. The pamphlet advocates a mixed economy in which state and private enterprises compete on equal terms, with the burden of proving the need for nationalisation resting on the Government.

## Key points

- The pamphlet restates the Swatantra Party’s 1967 opposition to bank nationalisation and argues that the Government failed to justify the measure.
- Masani presents social control and other regulatory measures as alternatives to state ownership.
- The pamphlet argues that nationalisation threatens monetary stability, savings, competition and depositor choice.
- It criticises the compensation offered to shareholders, citing undervalued property, unrecognised goodwill and inadequate interest.
- It argues that bank employees would lose collective-bargaining, strike and bonus rights.
- Small depositors are portrayed as vulnerable to politically directed and wasteful investment, with only the first Rs. 5,000 guaranteed.
- The pamphlet links nationalisation to concentration of economic power, monopoly and corruption, contrasting this with Gandhi’s preference for decentralisation.
