## Summary

These draft minutes record the Swatantra Party General Council meeting held in New Delhi on 9–10 June 1974, chaired by Piloo Mody. The meeting was convened to consider the proposed programme of a new national political alternative and the possible dissolution of existing parties. Members initially objected that the meeting had been given inadequate notice and that the 24-page draft programme had not circulated sufficiently in advance. Mody explained that negotiations among opposition leaders had led to the draft and argued that political circumstances required efforts to build a two-party system capable of checking the ruling Congress.

The discussion revealed a sharp division over whether the Swatantra Party should dissolve, merge into a new party, or retain its identity within a federation or electoral alliance. Supporters argued that many small parties were confusing voters, that the country was more important than party labels, and that a new formation could provide a stronger national alternative. Opponents warned that the proposal was premature, ideologically diluted, damaged by adverse publicity, and likely to bring the party into “wrong company.” Several members proposed a federal arrangement allowing parties to retain their names, flags, and symbols, while others insisted that democracy required a more decisive national opposition. The minutes conclude with resolutions welcoming the proposed new party and authorising a national convention, congratulating Jayaprakash Narayan’s non-violent Bihar struggle, and protesting the arrest of Ramender Kalia of Haryana.

## Key points

- The General Council met to debate a draft programme for creating a national political alternative and a possible two-party system.
- Piloo Mody defended the late circulation of the draft and described negotiations with leaders of several opposition parties.
- Members disagreed over dissolution and merger versus a federation or electoral alliance preserving existing party identities.
- Supporters presented consolidation as necessary to counter the ruling Congress and reduce voter confusion caused by numerous small parties.
- Opponents feared ideological compromise, loss of the Swatantra Party’s identity, weak organisational coherence, and association with unsuitable partners.
- Speakers linked the party’s decline to reduced membership, finances, electoral performance, public image, and distance from its original farmer-oriented character.
- The Council approved resolutions calling an early National Convention, supporting Jayaprakash Narayan’s Bihar struggle, and seeking an inquiry into Ramender Kalia’s arrest.
