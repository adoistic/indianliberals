summary: |-
  This English-language 1974 Swatantra Party office-paper dossier records an internal debate over whether the party should dissolve to facilitate a broader National Alternative. The correspondence between M. R. Masani and Piloo Mody shows a sharp disagreement over strategy. Masani supports bringing together non-Congress, non-Communist and non-Marxist parties, arguing that a common programme could create a credible two-party system and counter the ruling Congress. Mody accepts the need for a wider opposition but argues that dissolving Swatantra would sacrifice its distinctive principles and policies without guaranteeing ideological coherence, public credibility or national reach.
  
  The dossier also contains Lakshman Singh’s refusal to attend a convention he expects may liquidate the party, draft resolutions prepared for the August 4–5 National Convention, and competing proposals on the party’s future. The adopted resolution material endorses the creation of a National Alternative and the cessation of Swatantra as a political party, while preserving its principles through a non-party Swatantra Seva Sangh devoted to education and constructive work. A separate notice records opposition to dissolution, emphasizing that Swatantra remains the only national party among the proposed partners and that its principles remain valid. The rendered dossier therefore captures both the institutional case for dissolution and the dissenting case for continuity; the final convention outcome beyond the rendered pages is not available here.

## Key points

- Masani advocates dissolving Swatantra as part of a broader National Alternative comprising non-Congress, non-Communist and non-Marxist parties.
- Mody questions whether the proposed combination would be ideologically homogeneous, electorally credible or more effective than Swatantra operating independently.
- The correspondence debates whether a two-party system can be built while Congress (O) and Jan Sangh retain separate identities.
- Dissolution is presented in draft resolutions as a means of enabling members to join a new party while preserving Swatantra principles through a service organisation.
- The dossier includes an opposing resolution rejecting dissolution and warning that the proposed alternative may benefit the ruling Congress.
- The documents repeatedly invoke constitutional procedure, General Council authority and a National Convention as the proper forums for decision.
- The materials document a transition from electoral party politics toward education, social work and the preservation of a liberal political tradition.
