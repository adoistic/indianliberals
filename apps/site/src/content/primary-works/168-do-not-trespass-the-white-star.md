## Key points

- Government intervention in education should remain consistent with parents’ fundamental rights.
- Parents should retain the choice of schools and the medium of instruction for their children.
- Academic autonomy and freedom should protect universities’ decisions about teaching and institutional affairs.
- The compulsory neighbourhood-school proposal is presented as incompatible with freedom of choice and genuine equality.
- Article 30 of the Constitution is invoked to defend minorities’ rights to establish and administer educational institutions.
- Parliament and the Union Government are criticized for interfering in university education and the medium of instruction.
- Masani objects to political patronage, politicized textbook nationalisation, and the appointment of politicians to educational bodies.
