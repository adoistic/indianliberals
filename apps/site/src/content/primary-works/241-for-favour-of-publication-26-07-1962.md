## Summary

This two-page press release reports M. R. Masani’s address to the Progressive Group on the “Changing Pattern of Party Politics.” Masani presents India, after fifteen years of Jawaharlal Nehru’s administration, as facing economic crisis, violated frontiers, military defeat, and diplomatic isolation. He attributes the Congress’s continued power partly to the single-member constituency and simple-majority electoral system, which had allowed it to remain in office despite receiving only 45 per cent of the popular vote for Parliament, and calls for the consolidation of democratic opposition parties into a National Democratic Front.

Masani rejects the conventional classification of the Swatantra Party as “Rightist.” He describes its programme as a combination of Liberalism and Gandhism, grounded in minimum government, individual liberty, the dignity of the person, and the Fundamental Rights in the Constitution. The release concludes with proposals for strengthening opposition politics: advance selection of candidates, strict standards of competence and integrity, a trained cadre of workers, a broad social base, organisational discipline, and reliance on many small contributions rather than a few wealthy donors. It also stresses that political parties are instruments for serving the country rather than ends in themselves.

## Key points

- Masani diagnoses India as facing bankruptcy, violated frontiers, military defeat, and diplomatic isolation.
- He links the Congress’s continued rule to the single-member constituency and simple-majority electoral system.
- He urges the merger of democratic opposition parties into a National Democratic Front.
- He argues that the Swatantra Party’s emphasis on individual liberty and Fundamental Rights makes it liberal rather than conventionally Rightist.
- He identifies the party’s ideological sources as Liberalism and Gandhism, with minimum government and ordered progress.
- He proposes earlier candidate selection, high standards of integrity, a trained cadre, broad-based membership, organisational discipline, and small-donor financing.
- He rejects caste, money, local influence, and partisan loyalty as substitutes for public-minded and competent political leadership.
