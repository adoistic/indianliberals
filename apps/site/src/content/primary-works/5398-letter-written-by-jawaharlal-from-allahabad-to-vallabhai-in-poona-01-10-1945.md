## Key points

- Nehru defends maintaining friendly relations with Chiang Kai-shek and the Chinese Government despite disagreements with Chiang's critics.
- Azad questions whether Dr. G.V. Deshmukh's record of alleged indiscipline and criticism of Gandhi is compatible with a Congress candidacy.
- Patel describes a dispute over leadership of the Central Assembly party involving Sarat Bose and Asaf Ali, warning that outside pressure could provoke party trouble.
- The correspondence evaluates candidates partly through ability, nationalist commitment, prior Congress service, regional representation, and community balance.
- Patel and his correspondents debate whether Bombay's representation should include Maharashtrian, Gujarati, and Parsi candidates.
- Patel ultimately defends selecting Deshmukh as a practical electoral choice despite acknowledged faults.
- The final letters address campaign expenses, Congress financing, and the obligation to protect the party's prestige.
