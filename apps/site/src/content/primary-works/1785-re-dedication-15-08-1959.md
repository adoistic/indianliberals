## Summary

In this four-page address delivered at the Swatantra Party's Flag Salutation Ceremony on August 15, 1959, K. M. Munshi calls on party members to renew their commitment to the values associated with Indian independence: freedom, equality, human dignity, democracy, and constitutional government. He warns that these values are difficult to preserve amid utilitarianism, materialism, coercive state power, fraud, force, and declining respect for religion and moral principles. The Swatantra Party, led by C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji), is presented as a new political force formed in response to national frustration and opposed to socialism, totalitarianism, and the expansion of bureaucratic control.

The address then sets out a broad programme combining Gandhian democracy and non-violence with individual initiative, limited state compulsion, constitutional liberties, rule of law, judicial independence, and administrative integrity. Economically, it supports family farming, the self-employed sector, private enterprise, sound money, thrift in public expenditure, and economic freedom within defined social limits. The state should secure food, water, housing, clothing, social justice, labour protections, and equality of opportunity, but should not become the sole employer or control production and distribution. Munshi concludes by urging a return to Gandhi's teachings of truth, non-violence, national unity, moral values, and selfless service.

## Key points

- The speech marks Independence Day by urging Swatantra Party members to re-dedicate themselves to freedom, equality, human dignity, and democratic constitutionalism.
- It presents the Swatantra Party as opposed to socialism, totalitarianism, excessive state control, and the growth of bureaucracy.
- Its political ideal is Gandhian democracy, combining faith in the people, truth and non-violence, individual initiative, and limited state compulsion.
- It defends constitutional liberties, the rule of law, an independent judiciary, and judicial review.
- Its economic programme favours family farming, self-employment, private enterprise, sound money, fiscal restraint, and limited public-sector intervention.
- The address supports social justice, equality of opportunity, provision of basic needs, labour protections, and safeguards against unreasonable profits and prices.
- It frames moral and spiritual values, including Gandhi's teachings, as necessary foundations for political and economic development.
