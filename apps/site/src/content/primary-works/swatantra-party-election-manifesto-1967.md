# Swatantra Party Election Manifesto 1967

*Issued by the Swatantra Party*

## Summary

Swatantra Party Election Manifesto 1967 is the platform on which the Swatantra Party — India's classical-liberal opposition — contested the fourth general election. Its opening chapters indict twenty years of unbroken Congress rule as a demonstration of that party's "total unfitness to continue in power," surveying failure across four spheres: a national unity fractured by linguistic controversies, water disputes and territorial claims; a collapse of public morality into bribery, corruption, favouritism and a cynicism spreading among the young; an economy driven toward devaluation and near-bankruptcy by extravagant Plans and deficit finance; and an international policy of "continuous prattling about non-alignment" even as China and Pakistan violated India's borders. The Party emphatically rejects the argument that the remedy lies in more concentration of power — "Dictatorship or Statism in the name of Socialism" — insisting instead that significant progress comes only when the creative potential of individual endeavour and enterprise is released within the restraining framework of good government.

Recalling that some nine and a half million voters (about 8.25 per cent) had backed it in 1962, making it the leading Opposition in both Houses of Parliament and three state legislatures, the manifesto lists what its "small but gallant band" of legislators achieved: blunting the drive toward collective "Joint Cooperative Farming," delaying the Seventeenth Amendment's threat to confiscate peasants' land, opposing Gold Control, freeing food producers from Essential Commodities controls, securing tax reliefs, and seeing its warnings on the Third Plan, inflation and rupee devaluation vindicated. Its positive programme — the "Programme of Ordered Progress" — puts the common man first and rests on the inviolability of personal freedom and individual possessions, on economic power decentralised "as Gandhiji had envisaged," and on the principle that in a free and competitive society "the consumer is king."

The bulk of the document is a sector-by-sector policy sweep. Agriculture, treated as India's "Basic Industry," is to be freed from state controls through repeal of the Seventeenth Amendment, security of tenure and fair rents for tenants, abolition of land revenue, ample credit and inputs, and the farmer's right to the best free-market price backed by a guaranteed floor price. In industry and trade the Party opposes all monopoly, whether in the state or the free sector, and would halt further investment in loss-making public-sector plants; in labour it accepts trade unions and the right to strike while wanting unions run by workers rather than by political parties. It would scrap the proposed Fourth Plan, dissolve the Planning Commission as a "parallel Government unknown to the Constitution," and pursue "democratic planning by persuasion" on the French, British and Scandinavian model. On money it demands an "Honest Rupee" through an end to deficit finance and lower taxes, opposes the nationalisation of banks as fatal to the savings of small depositors, and would abolish Gold Control entirely.

Beyond economics, the manifesto pledges to restore the Fundamental Rights eroded by repeated constitutional amendments, to give the restoration of law and order the highest priority, to reform the electoral system to curb the influence of money power, and to establish an Ombudsman to check administrative injustice. In foreign affairs it declares that non-alignment has "lost all meaning" after Chinese and Pakistani aggression, calls for defence alliances with reliable powers against Communist China — "the principal menace to freedom in Asia" — supports the liberation of Tibet and recognition of the Dalai Lama, and backs South Vietnam against Chinese and North Vietnamese expansionism. On culture it champions parental choice in education, university autonomy, and a scientific and technical orientation, and — alongside the Rule of Law — the revival of Dharma as "a God-oriented inner law," together with the protection of minorities and Backward Classes, the eradication of untouchability, and the independence of the press from state control. It closes by offering voters "restrained and enlightened self-interest at the individual level and a service purpose at the State level" as the sure foundations for progress, and urges them to "Vote Swatantra" for a new government and a new policy.
