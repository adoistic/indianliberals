## Summary

This interview with Minoo Masani, dated 5 October 1966, presents the Swatantra Party as a mixed “liberal-conservative-peasants’” party rather than simply a conservative formation. Masani links its liberalism to competition, private enterprise, limited government, and constitutional democracy, while explaining its rural base through the small landholding peasantry. He argues that the state should provide essential infrastructure—water, irrigation, electricity, roads, transport, telecommunications, and technical training—while leaving the production of consumer goods and services primarily to private enterprise.

Masani attacks inefficient state monopolies, nationalisation, central planning, and large wasteful projects, using the Indian Railways and the Soviet Union as examples. He supports competition and selective privatisation where public enterprises are monopolistic, incompetent, or loss-making, but accepts minimum regulation against exploitation and other anti-social practices. On agriculture, he favours peasant proprietorship, smaller and more productive holdings, credit and irrigation for small farmers, and opposes transferring foodgrain farms to joint-stock corporations.

The interview also addresses electoral reform, party alliances, labour, Gandhian influence, socialism, communism, and foreign policy. Masani advocates proportional representation, expects Swatantra to build support among workers over the long term, describes the party as a fusion of Gandhian ideas with modern liberal principles, and defends a military victory over North Vietnam as the means of ending the Vietnam War. He distinguishes social justice from state socialism, endorsing decentralised competitive enterprise and Gandhian trusteeship while defending civil liberties and opposing preventive detention and indiscriminate bans on communist parties.

## Key points

- Masani describes Swatantra as a mixed liberal, conservative, and peasant party rather than an exclusively conservative party.
- The state should concentrate on infrastructure and essential public functions, while private enterprise should provide goods and services competitively.
- Masani criticises inefficient state monopolies, nationalisation, central planning, and wasteful public-sector projects.
- He supports peasant proprietorship, smaller landholdings, agricultural credit, irrigation, and opposition to collectivisation of foodgrain farms.
- He advocates proportional representation and argues that the existing electoral system distorts popular representation.
- The interview combines a defence of capitalism and competitive enterprise with minimum regulation, labour rights, and Gandhian trusteeship.
- Masani rejects appeasement of communist regimes and supports a decisive military response to North Vietnamese aggression.
