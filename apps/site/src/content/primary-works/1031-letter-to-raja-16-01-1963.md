## Summary

Written from Nidubrolu on 16 January 1963, this typed letter from N. G. Ranga (Member of Parliament, Lok Sabha) to C. Rajagopalachari carries opposition-party strategy across the aftermath of the Sino-Indian War. Ranga opens by hoping that their two days of discussions have not strained his older colleague, then reproduces verbatim a wire he has just received from Minoo Masani. The wire urges the Swatantra Party to table a parliamentary amendment condemning the Government's likely acceptance of the Colombo Proposals as a 'capitulation', to hold the line against negotiations until the Chinese vacate all occupied territory, and to move an adjournment motion over the arrest of three patriots.

Ranga tells Rajaji he has convened an informal meeting of the leaders of the Democratic Parties for the afternoon of the 20th in Delhi, wants Rajaji's advice to reach him by the 19th, and expects other opposition parties will float their own proposals that his meeting will have to weigh and, if possible, converge into a jointly signed formula. The letter's most searching passage is a strategic worry: the Press and Nehru's insistence on a Chinese withdrawal behind the 8 September line of actual occupation may create a public 'sense of relief' about a de facto pause in the war; how, Ranga asks, is the opposition to frame its position without appearing to fly in the face of India's own unpreparedness, or to invite censure from Non-Aligned countries as mere war-mongers? He closes on page two by noting he is leaving for Delhi the next morning by G. T. Express and will be staying at 44, Western Court, signing off 'Yours affectionately'.

## Key points

- Private letter from N. G. Ranga (MP, Lok Sabha) at Nidubrolu to Rajaji, dated 16 January 1963, in the wake of the Sino-Indian War and on the eve of the Third Lok Sabha's Budget Session.
- Reproduces a full telegram from Minoo Masani calling on the Swatantra Party to table an amendment opposing the Government's intended acceptance of the Colombo Proposals as a 'capitulation'.
- Masani's wire also pushes for an adjournment motion over the arrest of three patriots, framed as a follow-through on the party's opening-day resolution.
- Ranga has convened an informal meeting of Democratic Party leaders for the afternoon of 20 January in Delhi and asks Rajaji to send his advice by the 19th.
- Anticipates that other opposition parties will move their own notices and hopes the informal meeting can produce a jointly-signed 'agreed formula'.
- Diagnoses the political trap posed by the Press and Nehru's demand for Chinese withdrawal behind the 8 September line of actual occupation — a demand that could induce public relief and paint hard-line opposition voices as war-mongers before the Non-Aligned world.
- Confirms travel logistics: leaving for Delhi the next day by G. T. Express, staying at 44, Western Court.
