summary: |-
  Delivering the 2nd Indian Liberals Annual Lecture, Dr. Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) argues that while India's constitutional political liberties largely survive, its public discourse remains 'fundamentally still state-centric' and 'statism is still part of our DNA' even after 1991. He illustrates persistent economic illiberalism with the fierce resistance to the three farm laws, the forced rollback of Karnataka's move to double agricultural land-ceiling limits, pervasive land-transfer restrictions, the spreading 'freebie culture,' and states reverting to the old pay-as-you-go pension scheme that the Vajpayee government had replaced. He contends that pure liberalism has 'never succeeded politically anywhere in the world' (citing weak Free Democrats in Germany, protectionist drift among US Republicans, Brexit-era Britain, and Macron's pension fight in France), so liberals must 'leave this whole idea of economic liberalism around the notion of economic growth,' invoking Arvind Panagariya's point that one percentage point of growth over a decade is worth about $600 billion.

  Controversially for the audience, JP urges liberals to 'own' quality school education (accessible without out-of-pocket cost) and affordable healthcare, noting India spends only about 1% of GDP on health while roughly 60 million people fall into poverty each year from health costs, and warning that socialists occupy this moral high ground only because Indian liberals have ceded it. He identifies key growth hurdles: the Union's structural revenue deficit (~5.6 trillion rupees), weak rule of law and contract enforcement, poor education outcomes despite high per-child spending, and unplanned distress urbanization; and proposes a zero-revenue-deficit rule enforced via an independent fiscal body (like Britain's OBR), using Article 293 and the Finance Commission/CAG. In the Q&A he credits the Modi government for fiscal restraint during COVID while faulting it on rule of law and centralization, argues ideology is now 'market segmentation' and the real fault line is 'freebies versus growth,' calls for domain-specialist, competitive, up-or-out civil services, and advises against forming a new Swatantra-style party under first-past-the-post in favour of the think-tank model Hayek recommended to Antony Fisher.
