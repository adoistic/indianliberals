## Summary

This six-page correspondence packet records M. R. Masani’s opposition to dissolving the Swatantra Party in order to create a proposed non-Marxist opposition party. In the covering letter of 2 July 1974, Masani tells the party’s General Council that he is circulating his replies to Piloo Mody, the party president, after receiving enquiries from members who were absent from the Delhi meeting.

In his letter of 6 June, Masani accepts the principle of eventual consolidation among non-Marxist parties but argues that the proposed combination is too narrow, electorally weak, and insufficiently coherent to constitute a national alternative. He proposes retaining Swatantra’s identity while cooperating with other parties through a common programme for “Unity in Action,” postponing dissolution until a genuinely broad and principled alternative emerges. In the letter of 1 July, he challenges Mody’s claim that political actors must join the Congress or a National Alternative or retire, questions the credibility and record of the proposed partners, defends Swatantra’s principles as the only clear alternative to statist policies, and offers a further proposal: transforming Swatantra into a service or constructive-work organisation while preserving its name and principles. The packet ends before any response or final decision is shown.

## Key points

- Masani circulated his correspondence to the Swatantra Party’s General Council on 2 July 1974.
- He opposed dissolving Swatantra merely to facilitate a new coalition of non-Marxist parties.
- He regarded the proposed coalition as too small, regionally limited, and politically heterogeneous to be a credible national alternative.
- He argued that a new party required a clear reversal of statist economic policies rather than only a vague statement of principles.
- He proposed preserving Swatantra’s identity while cooperating with other parties through a common programme for “Unity in Action.”
- He offered transforming Swatantra into a service or constructive-work organisation that would retain its name and principles.
- He maintained that Swatantra’s disappearance would weaken the country’s only explicit alternative to statist policies.
