summary: |-
  Subodh Shenoy narrates the early life of his father, the economist B.R. Shenoy, framing it as both a personal and a national story. Born one of around eleven children of a farmer in the northern Kerala village of Bellicote, the young Raghunath was swept up in the independence movement led by Gandhi, ran away from home before finishing school, and was arrested and jailed by the British for carrying the Congress flag in Bombay. Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya, needing a boy to cook and clean, took him in and encouraged him to finish his schooling and come to the university he had founded.

  Shenoy recounts how his father made his way to Banaras Hindu University, where Malaviya remembered him and granted him full scholarships for undergraduate and MA studies in economics, in which he was a topper. He then won a scholarship to the London School of Economics for a BSc Econ, where Friedrich Hayek was among his teachers, and published one of the first papers by an Indian in a refereed international economics journal before returning to India.
