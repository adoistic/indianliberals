## Summary

In the rendered page, M. R. Masani argues that Indian democracy requires more than periodic elections: it depends on equal political opportunity, clean electoral administration, responsible parties, and an informed public. He criticises the prevailing electoral system for excessive expenditure, misuse of official machinery, and the influence of money and organised interests, while welcoming proposed reforms and calling for their implementation.

In the rendered page, Masani also discusses the role of the media and the need to protect freedom of expression. His concrete proposal is for constituency-level citizens’ councils, intended to act as impartial observers of elections and help prevent coercion, corruption, and other abuses. The page is the second instalment of a serial newspaper article; the first instalment and any continuation beyond this page were not available as part of the article itself.

## Key points

- Masani links democratic quality to equality of opportunity and fair elections.
- He identifies excessive electoral expenditure and the misuse of official machinery as problems in the electoral system.
- He argues that election-related reforms should be implemented rather than left as recommendations.
- He treats the media as important to democratic accountability and discusses restrictions on mass communication.
- He proposes citizens’ councils in constituencies to observe elections impartially.
- The proposal is intended to guard against coercion, corruption, and other improper electoral practices.
