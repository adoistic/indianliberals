summary: |-
  Deepak Lal recalls that after his disillusioning stint at the Planning Commission he returned to teaching at University College London, where the Institute of Economic Affairs was one of the few classical-liberal institutions in England at the time. Attending its meetings and lunches, he met F. A. Hayek, Milton Friedman, and others, read their work, and found in it an alternative view to central planning that he decided 'might work'. He describes this as the turning point in his thinking.

  He adds a methodological anecdote: Hayek once told him that although Milton Friedman was on the same side politically, Friedman was a 'fine tuner' who did not believe in econometrics, so the two were not methodologically aligned. Lal notes that by this period Hayek had largely moved away from economics into political philosophy, having recently completed The Constitution of Liberty and related works.
