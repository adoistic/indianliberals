# The High-Caste Hindu Woman

*By Pandita Ramabai*

## Summary

The High-Caste Hindu Woman is Pandita Ramabai Sarasvati's pioneering English-language book, first published in 1887 in Philadelphia while she was in the United States. Written by one of nineteenth-century India's most remarkable women scholars and reformers, it is an unsparing indictment of the condition of women in orthodox high-caste Hindu society — and one of the earliest sustained arguments for their emancipation made by an Indian woman to an international audience.

Drawing on Ramabai's own learning in Sanskrit scripture and on her lived observation, the book documents the successive stages of a high-caste woman's life — girlhood, child marriage, wifehood, and above all the enforced, degraded condition of the child-widow — and argues that these customs rest on social and priestly authority rather than on any defensible moral foundation. It was written in part to raise support for the education and rescue of Hindu widows, work Ramabai went on to institutionalise through her Sharada Sadan.

Within the archive the book stands as a foundational liberal-reformist text: a case for the dignity, education, and individual rights of the person against the weight of inherited custom.
