**Speaker 2** (00:10):
while laying down the foundations for India's subsequent feminist movement. Rukmabai was born on the 22nd of November, 1864 in Mumbai, at a time when the Indian society was still at odds with the concept of women's rights and liberty. Married at the tender age of 11, Rukmabai defied the norm by refusing to stay with her husband Bikaji. This marked the beginning of an era-defining case, the Dadaji Bikaji vs Rukmabai case. The case began with her husband's demand for restitution of conjugal rights. While the case proceedings went on, she resided in her maternal home and wrote extensive articles on infant marriage and forced widowhood. Rukmabai fought relentlessly for her freedom from unfair practices associated with child marriage. When asked to choose between living with her husband or facing imprisonment for six months, Rukmabai boldly chose incarceration. At last, after Rukmabai's appeal to Queen Victoria, the final verdict was declared in her favor and her marriage with Bikaji was dissolved. Rukmabai's landmark victory initiated a series of inspiring actions taken to safeguard the autonomy of Indian women. One such example is the Age of Consent Act 1891, which raised the age of consent for sexual intercourse for all girls from 10 to 12 years. After breaking from the shackles of her oppressive marriage, Rukmabai devoted her life to improving the perilous state of women's healthcare in India. She pursued a degree in medicine from University of London and became the first practicing female doctor in British India. Rukmabai is still remembered for her thunderous voice questioning the norm and for her awe-inspiring work in areas of medicine and social justice.
