## Summary

The Swatantra Party Manifesto, issued under the slogan “To Prosperity Through Freedom,” presents the party as a democratic opposition to Congress rule and to what it describes as increasingly centralized, interventionist, and inefficient government. It argues that India needs clean administration, restoration of constitutional freedoms, stronger Parliament, greater state autonomy, independent public services, electoral reform, and an opposition capable of replacing the ruling party through elections. The manifesto also links political liberty to individual enterprise, property rights, and freedom from excessive regulation and taxation.

Its economic programme gives priority to agriculture, peasant proprietorship, small-scale and rural industry, employment, stable prices, reduced taxation, and opposition to collective farming and nationalisation. Government is assigned a regulatory and enabling role rather than a direct role in production and trade. The manifesto rejects Soviet-style centralised planning, supports private enterprise and competition, calls for expanded exports without indiscriminate import compression, and advocates foreign investment while warning against excessive foreign debt. Later sections extend the programme to workers’ rights, constitutional government, press freedom, religious freedom, education, minority and backward-class protections, defence, and a firmer policy toward Communist China. The conclusion frames the party’s purpose as preventing one-party rule and protecting democracy.

## Key points

- The manifesto calls for a strong democratic opposition to Congress and condemns one-party dominance, administrative inefficiency, corruption, and arbitrary government.
- It presents individual freedom, constitutional government, parliamentary supremacy, federalism, judicial independence, and civil liberties as foundations of political life.
- Its economic programme prioritises peasant proprietorship, agricultural productivity, small-scale industry, employment, stable prices, and lower taxation.
- The party opposes collective farming, nationalisation, monopolies, excessive controls, and centralised Soviet-style planning.
- Government is assigned an enabling and regulatory role in industry and trade, with emphasis on competition, private enterprise, employee ownership, and balanced development.
- The manifesto supports workers’ organisation and collective bargaining while linking wage growth to productivity and national prosperity.
- It advocates independent public services, electoral reform, press freedom, religious freedom, autonomous education, and stronger protections for minorities and backward classes.
- In foreign affairs it rejects non-alignment as insufficient after Chinese territorial aggression, calls for a firmer policy toward China, and supports exports and carefully managed foreign capital.
