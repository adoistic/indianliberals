## Key points

- The article attributes President–Prime Minister clashes to unresolved constitutional questions about presidential discretion.
- It recounts Rajendra Prasad’s disagreements with Nehru over ministerial advice, the President’s powers, and the treatment of Defence Minister Krishna Menon.
- Prasad is described as defending the President’s independent constitutional position, while Nehru is portrayed as expecting the President to follow cabinet advice.
- The article argues that later Presidents became increasingly dependent on Prime Ministers and were repeatedly selected for pliability.
- It criticises the succession of presidents whom the article characterises as 'rubber stamps', including V. V. Giri and Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed.
- The conclusion asks whether Indian democracy can elect an independent President rather than continue the cult of personality and cabinet dominance.
