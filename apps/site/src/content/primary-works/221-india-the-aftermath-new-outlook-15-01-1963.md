  This visible four-page extract from New Outlook, a Liberal Magazine, No. 15 (January 1963), brings together commentary on the aftermath of the Sino-Indian War, India's foreign-policy choices, urban planning, and education. M. R. Masani argues that the 1962 conflict exposed failures in governmental preparedness and foreign-policy judgment, while insisting that India must build military strength, secure allies, and repair relations with Pakistan. The issue's liberal perspective links national security to democratic accountability and criticises Communist China and the Nehru government's handling of the crisis.
  
  The other visible articles extend the issue's concerns into domestic policy. The planning article advocates comprehensive, state-led regional planning for metropolitan growth while warning against both administrative fragmentation and indiscriminate laissez-faire. Lyndon Jones's education article argues for practical reform of primary, secondary, and higher education, including better teacher training, more flexible curricula, and expanded higher-education opportunities.
