## Key points

- The notes frame the Chinese offensive as part of a wider communist strategy linking Berlin, Cuba, Vietnam, and NEFA.
- They argue that diagnosis must precede policy, while withholding judgment on the COC’s decisions.
- They call for national unity, solidarity, discipline, and voluntary cooperation against antisocial economic practices.
- They distinguish democratic unity from totalitarian unity, using Britain versus Hitler and Stalin as contrasting examples.
- They defend freedom of expression, criticism, free thinking, discussion, a free press, and an active opposition.
- They distinguish support for the war effort from unconditional support for government policies.
- They criticize India’s China policy and advocate relations with the Chinese Nationalist government to create a two-front challenge for Mao.
- They argue that patriotic parties should oppose policies and officials responsible for military weakness.
